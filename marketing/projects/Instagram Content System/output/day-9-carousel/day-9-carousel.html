The Definition

One farm, one region, one country.

What it means

Single origin simply means your coffee comes from one specific place: a single farm, a cooperative, or one region. Nothing else is mixed in. So you can actually trace where it grew, who grew it, and how it was processed.

A blend mixes beans from a few different origins instead, and that is not a bad thing at all, just a different style.

Why it matters

Where it grows changes everything about how it tastes.

Terroir

Altitude, soil, rainfall, and temperature all shape the flavour. A coffee grown at 1,800m in Ethiopia tastes nothing like one grown at 900m in Sumatra, even though both are Arabica. That difference is what coffee people call terroir, the same idea wine people have been using for ages.

And yes, we borrowed the word from wine. No, we are not even a little bit sorry.

Real Examples

Three origins, three completely different cups.

Ethiopia
Floral, fruity, and very jasmine-forward, with a bright acidity. Honestly, it can taste more like a fragrant tea than the coffee you are used to.
Brazil
Nutty, chocolatey, low in acidity, with a full body. This is the one that makes your espresso taste like melted chocolate.
Yemen
Dark fruit, wine-like, and a little spiced. Intensely complex, and still grown the traditional way it has been for centuries.

The Point

Knowing the origin helps you predict the taste.

Practical use

Once you realise you enjoy a washed Kenyan (bright, berry-like, clean), you can look for that specifically instead of buying blind. Origin is basically your shortcut to finding what you actually like, without having to try twenty bags first.

You don't have to memorise every country lah. Just start paying attention to what you enjoy.
