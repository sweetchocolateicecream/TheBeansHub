The Myth

Dark roast is stronger. Light roast is weaker.

Actually

Roast level describes flavour development, not caffeine content or intensity. Dark roast tastes bolder because the roasting process produces smoky, bitter compounds — not because it has more caffeine. Light roast actually retains slightly more caffeine because the bean hasn't been roasted as long.

Dark roast just tastes like it should have more caffeine. The taste is lying to you. Classic.

Light Roast

Bright, fruity, complex.

What you get

High acidity, floral and fruit notes, lighter body. The origin flavours come through most clearly here because the bean hasn't been developed to a point where roast flavours take over. Best for pour-over and filter methods where you want to taste the bean itself.

Light roast is the one that tastes "weird" the first time, then becomes the one you can't stop thinking about.

Medium Roast

Balanced. The crowd pleaser.

What you get

Moderate acidity, more body than light roast, caramel and chocolate notes starting to come through. Works across multiple brew methods. Approachable for people transitioning from commercial coffee. Origin still present but softened by roast development.

Medium roast is the correct answer when someone asks "what should I buy first." No shame in it at all.

Dark Roast

Bold, smoky, full-bodied.

What you get

Low acidity, heavy body, dominant roast flavours — dark chocolate, smoke, bittersweet. The origin character is mostly gone at this point. What you taste is the roast itself. Best for espresso, milk-based drinks, or anyone who genuinely wants that bold, traditional coffee flavour.

Dark roast is valid. Just know you're mostly tasting the roaster's work, not the origin. Both are interesting.
