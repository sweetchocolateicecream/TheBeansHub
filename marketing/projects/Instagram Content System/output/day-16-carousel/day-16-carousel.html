Reason 01

Different roasters have completely different styles.

The Reality

Two roasters can buy the same Ethiopian beans and produce completely different cups. Roast profile, batch size, resting time — all of it shifts the flavour. Buying from one roaster means tasting one interpretation of coffee. There are dozens more.

The bean is the ingredient. The roaster is the chef. You wouldn't eat at the same restaurant forever.

Reason 02

Freshness windows don't align with loyalty.

The Reality

Specialty beans are freshest between 7 and 30 days after roasting. Most roasters batch-roast weekly or biweekly. If your roaster's restock doesn't align with when you run out, you either wait or you buy stale. A second or third roaster means you always have something fresh.

Freshness is not a preference. It is the difference between a good cup and a flat one.

Reason 03

Different price points, different moods.

The Reality

A RM28 bag for a weekday morning cup and a RM65 bag for a slow Saturday morning are both valid choices. Most single roasters don't span that range well — they have a house style and a price tier. Multiple roasters give you options that match how you're actually drinking.

The RM65 bag is not twice as good. But it is noticeably different. Both have their place.
