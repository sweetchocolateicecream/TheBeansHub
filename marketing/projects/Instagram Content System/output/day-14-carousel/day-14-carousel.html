Lesson 01

Buy small bags more often, not one big bag less often.

Why

A 1kg bag sounds economical. In practice, it goes stale before you finish it. 200–250g every two to three weeks gives you fresher coffee and the chance to try something different each time.

The RM savings on bulk do not survive contact with two-month-old coffee.

Lesson 02

Check the roast date. Not the best-before date.

Why

A roast date tells you how fresh the coffee actually is. Best-before dates are often set 12–18 months out, which is technically not expired, but specialty beans are best consumed within 4–8 weeks of roasting. One number matters. The other is just compliance.

If there's no roast date on the bag, that tells you something too.

Lesson 03

You don't need better gear. You need better technique.

Why

Consistent grind size and water temperature will improve your cup more than buying a better kettle. Most beginner coffee problems are solved by slowing down, not by spending more. The gear doesn't think for you.

We still bought the kettle anyway. No regrets. But it wasn't the fix.

Lesson 04

If it tastes sour, you probably extracted too little. If bitter, too much.

Why

Sour = under-extracted. Grind finer or brew longer. Bitter = over-extracted. Grind coarser or brew shorter. This simple framework fixes about 80% of bad home brew cups without changing the beans or the method.

Took us an embarrassingly long time to learn this. Now you can skip that part.

Lesson 05

Try beans from more than one roaster.

Why

Different roasters have different styles — even with the same origin. One roaster's Ethiopian might be bright and floral. Another's could be heavier and more fruit-forward. You don't know what you like until you've compared. It's also just more interesting.

This is also basically the reason The Beans Hub exists.
