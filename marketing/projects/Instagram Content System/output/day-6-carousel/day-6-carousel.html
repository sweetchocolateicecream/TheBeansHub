Flavour

In Vietnam, they call it Cà phê mít.Jackfruit Coffee.

Where it lives on the wheel

Arabica lives in floral and fruity. Robusta settles into nutty and cocoa. Liberica goes somewhere else entirely — spice, herbal, savoury, umami. The territories that most coffees never visit.

Pull up a flavour wheel. Find the section that doesn't look like coffee. That's where Liberica is.

The Bean

Arabica ripens in 6–8 months.Liberica takes 9–11.

That extra time is everything. The bean sits inside thick fruit pulp for longer, soaking up sugars and aromatic compounds as the cherry slowly breaks down around it.

The jackfruit note you taste in the cup is not an accident. It is the record of that waiting.

The Tree

Liberica grows where Arabica would die.

Height
18–20m
Four times taller than Arabica
Soil
Any
Peat, clay, acidic — the land farmers write off

The leaves are large, thick, and waxy. They hold moisture. They keep working in direct sunlight when other species would struggle.

Economics

For most of its history, farmers walked away from Liberica.

The old problem

The trees grew too tall. Harvesting required ladders and twice the labour. Ten kilograms of fresh cherries produced only about one kilogram of green beans — where Arabica gives you five or six.

Today, specialty-processed Liberica sells at 3–4× the price of Robusta. The economics have turned around completely.
