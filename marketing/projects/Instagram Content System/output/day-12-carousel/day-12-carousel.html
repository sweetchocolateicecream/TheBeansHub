Method 01

Washed process.

What happens

The fruit is removed from the bean before drying. The bean is then washed clean and dried on raised beds. Result: clean, bright, and high in acidity. The origin flavours come through clearly because there's no fruit fermentation to muddy them.

If you love Ethiopian coffees that taste like jasmine or bergamot, washed processing is usually why.

Method 02

Natural process.

What happens

The whole coffee cherry is dried intact — fruit still on the bean. As it dries, the fruit ferments and the sugars transfer into the bean. Result: fruity, heavy, complex. Often tastes like blueberries, wine, or dark chocolate. Very distinct.

Natural process is the chaotic one. High reward, but also more room for things to go wrong.

Method 03

Honey process.

What happens

The skin is removed but some of the mucilage (sticky fruit layer) is left on the bean during drying. It sits in the middle — more fruit character than washed, more clarity than natural. Sweetness is the signature. No actual honey involved.

Named for the sticky texture during drying, not the flavour. Slightly disappointing the first time you find this out.
