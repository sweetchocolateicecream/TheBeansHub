Arabica

Arabica is complex, nuanced, and expensive to grow.

The Profile

Lower caffeine, higher acidity, more complex flavour — fruity, floral, caramel, bright. Grows at high altitude in cooler climates. More susceptible to disease and climate change. This is what specialty coffee roasters primarily use, and why Malaysian specialty beans are almost entirely Arabica.

Most of what you'll find on The Beans Hub is Arabica. Not because Robusta is bad — it isn't. More on that.

Robusta

Robusta has nearly twice the caffeine and costs less to produce.

The Profile

Higher caffeine, lower acidity, heavier body. Earthy, rubbery, sometimes bitter if not processed well. Grows at low altitude and is hardier than Arabica. Often dismissed as inferior — which is unfair. The problem is usually poor processing, not the bean itself.

Vietnamese kopi and your traditional kopitiam coffee? Often Robusta. You've been drinking it your whole life.

When Robusta Wins

Three situations where Robusta is the correct answer.

Espresso Blends
Robusta adds crema, body, and caffeine kick. Many Italian espresso blends use 20–40% Robusta. The result is richer and denser.
Milk Drinks
Robusta's boldness cuts through milk better than a delicate light-roast Arabica. If you're making a kopi-o or a milky espresso, Robusta holds its character.
Caffeine
If you genuinely need more caffeine and taste is secondary, Robusta is a logical choice. Honest and practical.

The Real Story

The better vs worse framing is too simple.

The nuance

High-quality processed Robusta from Vietnam or Uganda can be genuinely interesting. The reputation problem is mostly from commodity-grade Robusta in cheap instant coffee blends. Just like bad Arabica tastes terrible. Quality and processing matter more than species. The species is just the starting point.

Malaysia actually grows Liberica too — a third species almost nobody talks about. Different conversation for another day.
