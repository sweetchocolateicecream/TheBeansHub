On the bag: Roast Date

The roast date is the most important number on the bag.

What to look for

Specialty coffee is freshest 7–30 days after the roast date. Before 7 days, the CO₂ hasn't fully off-gassed and the flavour is muted. After 4–6 weeks, the coffee starts going flat. No roast date = no transparency. Move on.

Best-before dates mean almost nothing for specialty coffee. Roast date is the real signal.

On the bag: Origin

Origin and altitude predict the flavour profile.

What it means

Ethiopia = floral, fruity, tea-like. Kenya = berry, bright, high acidity. Brazil = nutty, chocolatey, low acidity. Colombia = balanced, caramel, approachable. Higher altitude generally = more complex, brighter acidity. This is the shortcut to knowing what you're buying.

You don't need to memorise all of this. Just start noticing what origin you keep coming back to.

On the bag: Everything else

Four more things worth noticing.

Process
Washed = clean + bright. Natural = fruity + heavy. Honey = sweet + balanced.
Tasting Notes
Not a guarantee — they're a flavour roadmap. Suggests what to look for when you drink it.
Roast Level
Light = brightness + origin. Medium = balance. Dark = roast character + body.
Varietal
Gesha, Bourbon, SL28 — the bean variety. Less important to learn first, but becomes interesting later.

Putting it together

What a good bag actually tells you.

Example

Ethiopia Yirgacheffe · Washed · Light Roast · Roasted 12 days ago · Notes: jasmine, lemon, peach. That's a clean, bright, floral cup with high acidity. Best on a slow morning with a V60. You now know all of that without tasting it first. That's what the bag is for.

Once this clicks, buying coffee blind feels genuinely strange. There's so much information there already.
