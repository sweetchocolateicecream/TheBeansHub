# Ideal Customer Profile — The Beans Hub

## Primary Persona: The Curious Home Brewer

### Who They Are

**Age:** 22–35  
**Location:** Urban Malaysia — KL, Penang, JB primarily  
**Coffee relationship:** Somewhere between "I've moved past Nescafé" and "I own a grinder and have opinions about extraction ratios." They care about what they're drinking but don't need to be the most knowledgeable person in the room.

They brew at home — at minimum a pourover or Aeropress, possibly a moka pot or espresso machine. They buy whole beans. They've bought from 1–2 roasters before and they're open to trying more, but finding new ones takes effort they haven't bothered with yet.

---

### Psychographics

- Genuinely curious about flavour — they notice when a coffee tastes different and want to understand why
- Values local and intentional over big brand and convenient; would rather buy from a Malaysian roaster than a supermarket shelf if the process is easy
- Mildly intimidated by hardcore specialty coffee culture — the jargon, the gear gatekeeping, the Instagram accounts that feel like homework — but won't admit it
- Shares finds with friends; if they discover something great, they tell people (high word-of-mouth potential)
- Comfortable buying online; uses Shopee/Lazada but finds them frustrating for niche searches

---

### Spectrum Within This Persona

The audience spans two overlapping groups that can be addressed with the same content:

**Beginner-curious:** Got into home brewing 6–18 months ago. Has a basic setup. Buys from one or two roasters they found through Instagram or a friend. Wants to explore more but doesn't know where to start.

**Enthusiast:** Has been brewing 2+ years. Knows what they like (specific origins, processing methods). Actively seeks out new roasters. Frustrated by how fragmented the local scene is to navigate.

Both are ideal. Don't over-segment — content that works for one tends to work for the other.

---

### Buying Trigger

**Primary:** They finish a bag of beans and it's time to buy again. This is a habitual, recurring moment — not a one-time event. The decision of what to buy next is made in a low-effort, "what should I try this time?" mindset.

**Secondary triggers:**
- A friend mentions or posts about a roaster they haven't heard of
- They have a great cup somewhere (café, a friend's place) and want to find something similar
- They get new brewing equipment and want to experiment with different bean profiles

**The insight:** The purchase trigger is frequent and low-friction. The barrier isn't motivation — it's awareness and discoverability. They would explore more if it were easy. The platform's job is to be there and be useful at the exact moment they're deciding what to buy next.

---

### Objections & Hesitations

| Objection | What's underneath it | How to address it |
|---|---|---|
| "I've never heard of this platform" | Pure awareness gap — not distrust | Reach and frequency; show up where they already are |
| "I already have a roaster I like" | Habit and low switching cost | Frame as exploration, not replacement — "try alongside what you already like" |
| "I don't know if the quality is real" | No social proof yet | Surface real community picks, roaster stories, and taste descriptions that feel specific and credible |
| "Too much to choose from" | Discovery paralysis | Lead with filtered entry points: "best for beginners," "most popular this month," "similar to X" |

---

### Where They Spend Time Online

- **Instagram** — follows specialty coffee accounts, Malaysian lifestyle content, local food and beverage brands
- **Xiaohongshu (Red Note)** — increasingly popular for lifestyle discovery among urban Malaysian Chinese; used for café recommendations, product reviews, aesthetic content
- **YouTube** — passively consumes coffee brewing tutorials, gear reviews, origin stories
- **WhatsApp/Telegram groups** — coffee enthusiast communities, local FB groups (but this is word-of-mouth territory, not paid)

**Content formats that resonate:**
- Short-form video with honest, specific recommendations (not polished ads)
- Comparison posts: "if you like X, try Y"
- Roaster spotlights with a personal angle
- Educational bite-sizes: what a taste note actually means, how roast level affects flavour

---

### What Converts Them

They don't need to be convinced that specialty coffee is worth it — they're already there. What moves them is **a specific recommendation they can act on immediately.** Name a bean, name a roaster, tell them what it tastes like, tell them where to find it. That's the content that drives platform visits.
