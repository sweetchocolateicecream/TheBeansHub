# Brand Context — The Beans Hub

## What It Is

The Beans Hub is a specialty coffee discovery and marketplace platform focused exclusively on coffee beans roasted in Malaysia. It lets users search, filter, and find beans by taste notes, roast level, origin, and roaster — cutting through the chaos of general marketplaces like Shopee and Lazada where coffee competes for shelf space with electronics and household goods.

**The one thing it's building toward:** becoming the go-to destination for anyone in Malaysia who wants to find the right specialty coffee bean.

---

## Business Overview

- **Stage:** Early traction (~1 month live), pre-revenue
- **Model:** Two-sided marketplace. Phase 1 — grow a critical mass of consumer traffic. Phase 2 — monetise by charging roasters (listing fees, subscriptions, or marketing services) once audience size makes the platform worth paying for.
- **Geography:** Malaysia-first. All roasters on the platform are Malaysian.

---

## Positioning

**Problem it solves:** Specialty coffee beans in Malaysia are fragmented across individual roasters' Instagram pages, websites, and general marketplaces with no consistent filtering. Discovering a new roaster or finding a bean by taste profile is genuinely difficult.

**Core value proposition:** One organised, searchable place to find every specialty coffee bean roasted in Malaysia — filtered the way coffee drinkers actually think (taste notes, roast level, origin, etc.).

**Why it wins over alternatives:**
- Shopee/Lazada: Cluttered, unfiltered, no curation — coffee listed next to vacuum cleaners
- Individual roaster sites/Instagram: Siloed; requires knowing the roaster already exists
- Word of mouth: Unreliable, limited reach

The Beans Hub offers discoverability at a category level no single roaster or general marketplace can provide.

---

## Competitive Landscape

No dominant competitor exists in this niche in Malaysia. A few small attempts are out there but none have built meaningful traction or brand recognition. The bigger competitive risk is inertia — people are used to buying from one roaster they already know and have no strong reason to explore unless something prompts it.

---

## Current Priorities (This Quarter)

1. **Grow audience** — drive consistent traffic from coffee consumers to the platform via Instagram and Xiaohongshu (Red Note/Xiaohongshu)
2. **Convert visitors to repeat users** — get people who land on the platform to come back (currently no email capture or retention system in place)

Known gaps: inconsistent content posting cadence; no email list; no SEO strategy yet.

---

## Marketing Rules

1. **Traffic before monetisation.** Every campaign, post, and piece of content should be evaluated by one metric right now: does it bring the right person to the platform? Don't pitch to roasters publicly — that's a later conversation.
2. **Awareness is the only real funnel problem.** People who find The Beans Hub love it. The job of marketing is reach, not persuasion. Prioritise content that puts the platform in front of people who've never heard of it.
3. **Never compete on Shopee's terms.** Don't market like a general e-commerce listing. The edge is curation and specialisation — lean into that contrast explicitly.
4. **Celebrate local roasters visibly.** Feature them by name, story, and craft. This builds the supply side organically and gives consumers a reason to care beyond just price.
5. **Educate consistently.** Every post should either teach something (taste notes, brewing, origins) or surface a discovery (new roaster, new bean). Education builds trust and extends reach without feeling salesy.
6. **Build the email list as soon as possible.** Social reach is rented. A list of people who've opted in to hear about new beans and roasters is the only owned channel. Treat building it as urgent.
7. **Platform credibility compounds.** The more roasters listed, the more useful the platform. Prioritise content and outreach that encourages roasters to submit listings — the catalogue depth is the product.
