# 30 Series Ideas — TikTok & Instagram

**Companion to `For a series on TikTok and Instagram.docx`.** That doc is ONE series (the "AI replaced my job" build-in-public arc). This doc is 30 *distinct series* you can run in parallel or rotate.

## Read this first

- **Two audiences, tagged on every series below:**
  - **[A] The Beginner** — drinks latte at home daily, knows nothing (blend vs single origin, process, origin), and is *not researching*. Do NOT lecture. Let them *overhear* their own taste and feel smart for "discovering" it. Win = "oh, I'm a blend person and that's fine."
  - **[B] The Browser** — already knows their taste (usually by taste notes, sometimes origin/roast). Wants to window-shop the shelf. Win = they park on The Beans Hub and scroll 850 beans.
- **Your existing series is neither A nor B** — it pulls tech/career people. Keep it (it grows raw follower count and it's authentic to you), but it is NOT a coffee-buyer engine. Treat it as top-of-funnel *attention*, and route those people into an A or B series.
- **Banking = repeatable format + a reason to watch the next one.** Every series below has a fixed template so you can batch-film 8 at a time, and a built-in "follow for the next" mechanic.
- **Pick 3 to start.** Suggested starter set is flagged ⭐. Run 8 episodes of each, kill whatever dies by episode 4.

Legend: **⭐ = recommended starter** · **A / B = audience** · **Effort L/M/H**

## Part 1 — Beginner-magnet series ("accidental" discovery, no lecturing) — [A]

### 1. ⭐ "Which coffee person are you?" — [A] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Identity / self-categorisation. "You've been drinking coffee for years and never knew you're a ___ person."
- **Format:** 15s. You describe a drinker ("you add milk, you like it smooth, you buy whatever's on sale") → reveal the label ("congrats, you're a medium-roast blend drinker, and that's the smartest budget in coffee"). No jargon dumped — the label lands as flattery.
- **Content to use:** Your `taste_notes_category` set (Fruity, Nutty/Cocoa, Sweet, etc.) reframed as *personalities*, not data.
- **Why it banks:** People tag friends to assign them a type. Saves + shares. Beginners self-sort into a taste they'll later shop for.

### 2. ⭐ "Blend is not the cheap option, it's the smart option" — [A] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Permission / myth-relief. "You feel guilty buying blend. Stop."
- **Format:** You "confess" you drink blend daily, then justify it with one fact (blends are engineered for milk, consistency, price). Never say "let me teach you about blends."
- **Content to use:** Your price data — blends vs single-origin median. The RM35–40 milk-coffee tip from Idea 2 in your existing doc.
- **Why it banks:** Removes shame = strong emotional payoff = comment magnet. Converts guilt into a confident purchase (cheap, in-budget, "good enough" — exactly what they told you they want).

### 3. "Latte at home but make it slightly better" — [A] · Effort M
- **Hook type:** Tiny upgrade, zero effort. "One change to your home latte, no new gear."
- **Format:** Show the same latte they already make; swap ONE thing (fresher beans, a medium roast instead of supermarket dark). Taste reaction on camera.
- **Content to use:** Under-RM40 beans from the site. Their existing setup is the hero — you're not asking them to change their life.
- **Why it banks:** Low barrier = high completion. They see themselves in the video.

### 4. "Reading a coffee bag for the first time" — [A] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Relatable confusion. "This bag has 6 words I didn't understand. Now I do."
- **Format:** Hold a real bag, point at ONE label per episode (washed, natural, 1,600m, Nutty/Cocoa). Explain it in one sentence *as it applies to taste*, not theory.
- **Content to use:** Real roaster bags from your 52 roasters. One label = one episode = 20+ episodes of backlog.
- **Why it banks:** Micro-education disguised as "watch me be confused." No lecture energy. Endlessly repeatable.

### 5. "Things nobody tells you when you start drinking coffee" — [A] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Insider secret. "6 months in, here's what I wish someone told me on day one."
- **Format:** One overlooked truth per episode ("fresh beans have a smell that stale ones don't," "you don't need light roast if you drink milk").
- **Content to use:** Your own beginner-to-now story (you literally lived this — Dolce Gusto → fresh beans).
- **Why it banks:** "Wish I knew" framing = save-and-return. Positions you as the friend one step ahead, not the expert above them.

### 6. "I let a beginner pick beans by vibes only" — [A] · Effort M
- **Hook type:** Low-stakes experiment. "No knowledge allowed. Pick by the picture and the price."
- **Format:** You (or a friend) choose a bean using zero expertise — just the label vibe — then taste it. Sometimes it's great, sometimes not; both are content.
- **Content to use:** The site's browse/filter flow, filmed. Taste-note icons.
- **Why it banks:** Removes the fear that you need to "know things." Beginners realise the barrier is fake.

### 7. "Supermarket coffee vs a real roaster, blind" — [A] · Effort M
- **Hook type:** Contrast / stakes. "Can a beginner even taste the difference? Let's find out honestly."
- **Format:** Blind taste, honest reaction. Crucially — sometimes admit the cheap one is fine. Authenticity is the hook.
- **Content to use:** One local roaster bean vs their Nescafe/supermarket default.
- **Why it banks:** Beginners are the judge, not the student. If the roaster wins, they want in; if it's close, they trust you forever.

### 8. "Your budget coffee order, upgraded" — [A] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Reverse-flex / value. "You don't need RM100 beans. Here's what RM40 gets you."
- **Format:** Show 3 in-budget picks per episode with a one-line "who it's for."
- **Content to use:** The 231 beans under RM40 (from your existing doc). Filter live on the site.
- **Why it banks:** Pure value = the algorithm's favourite (saves). Directly matches "cheap n good n within my budget."

### 9. "Ranking beginner coffees by how easy they are to like" — [A] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Tier list / ranking. "S-tier to F-tier: which beans won't scare a beginner."
- **Format:** Tier-list graphic, drag beans into rows, quick reason each.
- **Content to use:** Crowd-pleaser beans from your catalogue (chocolatey, nutty, sweet — low acidity).
- **Why it banks:** Tier lists get screenshotted and argued with. Comments = reach.

### 10. "POV: you finally found your coffee" — [A] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Aspirational / emotional. "The moment you stop buying random beans and know what you like."
- **Format:** Cinematic, soft. The feeling of dialing in a taste you love. Light on info, heavy on vibe.
- **Content to use:** Your own "we hunt for specific taste notes now" arc.
- **Why it banks:** Sells the *destination* (having a taste identity). Pulls beginners toward wanting one — which is what The Beans Hub delivers.

## Part 2 — Browser-magnet series (window-shopping the shelf) — [B]

### 11. ⭐ "Prettiest coffee bags in Malaysia" — [B] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Eye candy / collection. "Roaster packaging tier list."
- **Format:** Fast montage of gorgeous bags from your 52 roasters, react/rank.
- **Content to use:** Bag photos (`image_url` in beans.json), your 52 roasters.
- **Why it banks:** [B] browsers love the *shelf*. This IS window shopping. Drives "where do I buy these" → site.

### 12. ⭐ "Every [taste note] coffee in Malaysia, ranked by price" — [B] · Effort M
- **Hook type:** Completeness / the whole shelf. "Every blueberry-forward bean, cheapest to priciest."
- **Format:** Scrolling leaderboard by taste note. One note = one episode.
- **Content to use:** `beans.json` filtered by `taste_notes_category`. This is literally your site's superpower on screen.
- **Why it banks:** [B] shops by taste note. You're showing them the exact filtered view — then telling them it's live on the site. Direct shelf-to-site handoff.

### 13. "The most expensive bean in Malaysia this week" — [B] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Extreme / curiosity. "RM740 for 200g. Who is this for?"
- **Format:** Recurring. Show the current top-price bean, break down why.
- **Content to use:** Your price data, sorted descending. Idea 7 from existing doc.
- **Why it banks:** Extremes travel. Browsers screenshot flexes. Recurring = follow mechanic.

### 14. "Hidden-gem roaster of the week" — [B] · Effort M
- **Hook type:** Discovery / insider. "You've never heard of this roaster and that's a crime."
- **Format:** Spotlight one small roaster, their story, their standout bean.
- **Content to use:** Your 52 roasters — *this doubles as backlink/relationship outreach* (CLAUDE.md flags roaster outreach as the #1 backlink play).
- **Why it banks:** Browsers crave undiscovered shelves. Roasters reshare = free distribution + eventual backlinks. Two birds.

### 15. "Coffee origin tier list (fight me in the comments)" — [B] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Opinion / bait. "Ethiopia S-tier, Brazil C-tier — argue."
- **Format:** Tier list of origins with spicy takes.
- **Content to use:** Your origin price/flavour breakdown (Idea 5 existing doc).
- **Why it banks:** [B] audience has opinions and WILL fight. Controversy = comments = reach.

### 16. "If your coffee taste was a personality" — [B] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Astrology-for-coffee. "Natural process = the chaos friend."
- **Format:** Match process/roast/origin to personality archetypes.
- **Content to use:** Process methods + taste categories from your data model.
- **Why it banks:** Browsers tag friends. Shareable, on-brand, no hard selling.

### 17. "Rating beans by their taste-note descriptions alone" — [B] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Judge-the-book. "'Notes of tomato and tobacco' — pass or buy?"
- **Format:** Read real (sometimes absurd) taste notes, react, guess if it's legit.
- **Content to use:** `taste_notes` field — some are genuinely wild and funny.
- **Why it banks:** Insider humour for [B]. They know these descriptions and love mocking/defending them.

### 18. "Building the ultimate Malaysian coffee shelf under RM300" — [B] · Effort M
- **Hook type:** Challenge / curation. "5 beans, RM300, cover every flavour zone."
- **Format:** Curate a starter cellar; justify each pick.
- **Content to use:** Cross-filter your catalogue by category + price.
- **Why it banks:** Browsers love a curated set. High save rate. Sends them to the site to build their own.

### 19. "Coffees that taste nothing like coffee" — [B] · Effort M
- **Hook type:** Surprise. "This one tastes like a fruit tea, not coffee."
- **Format:** Feature wild-flavour beans (fermented, funky naturals, Geisha).
- **Content to use:** Fruity/Sour-Fermented category beans.
- **Why it banks:** [B] chases novelty. Also nudges curious beginners upward.

### 20. "What your favourite café roasts (and where to buy it)" — [B] · Effort M
- **Hook type:** Café-to-home bridge. "Love this café? Their beans are on the shelf."
- **Format:** Connect popular Malaysian cafés/roasters to buyable beans.
- **Content to use:** Roasters in your catalogue that also run cafés.
- **Why it banks:** Turns café loyalty into home purchase. Very [B], high intent.

## Part 3 — Crossover / engine series (pull both, or feed the funnel)

### 21. ⭐ "I analysed 850 beans" data series — [A+B] · Effort M
- **Hook type:** Authority + curiosity (your existing strongest hook).
- **Format:** One data finding per episode (roast vs price, altitude vs price, origin leaderboard).
- **Content to use:** Ideas 1–8 already scripted in your existing doc — this is your best-built series, keep running it.
- **Why it banks:** The data cleanly serves both ICPs (beginners get "you're overpaying," browsers get the leaderboard). This is the bridge between your tech-audience series and coffee-buyer series.

### 22. "Taste-test the extremes" — [A+B] · Effort H
- **Hook type:** Stakes. "Cheapest bean vs most expensive, blind."
- **Format:** Honest side-by-side reactions.
- **Content to use:** RM16 Brazil vs RM740 Panama.
- **Why it banks:** Beginners learn price ≠ better; browsers get the flex tasting. Costs money though — batch it.

### 23. "Fixing beginner coffee mistakes" — [A] leaning crossover · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Gentle callout. "You're storing beans in the fridge. Stop."
- **Format:** One mistake per episode, no shame, quick fix.
- **Content to use:** Common home-brew errors (grind, water temp, storage, dark roast in milk).
- **Why it banks:** Practical + save-worthy. Browsers reshare to beginner friends.

### 24. "Guess the price" — [A+B] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Game. "Cheap bean or RM200 bean? You guess, I reveal."
- **Format:** Interactive; ask viewers to comment their guess before the reveal.
- **Content to use:** Your price dataset + bag photos.
- **Why it banks:** Comment-before-reveal = the single best engagement mechanic on short form.

### 25. "The coffee glossary you'll actually remember" — [A] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Anti-textbook. "One coffee word, explained with a real bean, in 15 seconds."
- **Format:** Washed, natural, honey, body, acidity — one per episode, tied to a bean you can buy.
- **Content to use:** Your data model fields + real catalogue examples.
- **Why it banks:** Beginners bookmark a whole series. Feeds SEO glossary content on the site too.

### 26. "Roaster reacts / roaster interview" — [B] leaning · Effort H
- **Hook type:** Access. "I asked a roaster the questions you can't."
- **Format:** Short interviews or DMs-to-video with your 52 roasters.
- **Content to use:** Roaster relationships — again doubles as outreach/backlinks.
- **Why it banks:** Roasters reshare to their audiences = distribution. Higher effort, high payoff.

### 27. "30 beans in 30 days" — [A+B] · Effort M
- **Hook type:** Challenge / streak. "One new Malaysian bean every day."
- **Format:** Daily quick tasting, ratchets a countdown.
- **Content to use:** Your catalogue breadth is the whole point.
- **Why it banks:** Streaks create daily-return habit and follows. Shows off catalogue depth.

### 28. "Coffee myths a data scientist debunks" — [A+B] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Myth-busting + your credibility. "'Expensive = better.' The data says no."
- **Format:** One myth, one data point, one verdict.
- **Content to use:** Price/altitude/roast correlations from your analysis.
- **Why it banks:** Myth-busting is inherently shareable; ties your data-scientist brand to coffee (the bridge you need).

### 29. "Window-shopping The Beans Hub live" — [B] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** ASMR-scroll / calm browse. "Just scrolling 850 beans, come vibe."
- **Format:** Screen-record filtering the site by mood/taste, soft narration.
- **Content to use:** The site itself — literally shows the product doing its job.
- **Why it banks:** [B] told you they want to *stay on the shelf*. This is that, and it drives direct traffic.

### 30. "Help me pick — you decide in the comments" — [A+B] · Effort L
- **Hook type:** Participation. "I'm buying one bean this week. You choose."
- **Format:** Present 2–3 options, let comments vote, film the follow-up tasting next episode.
- **Content to use:** Any 2–3 catalogue beans.
- **Why it banks:** Two-part payoff (vote → result) = guaranteed return viewer. Comments feed the algorithm.

## How to actually use this (don't run 30)

- **Start with the 6 ⭐:** #1, #2, #8 (beginner), #11, #12 (browser), #21 (data bridge). That covers both ICPs + your existing strength.
- **Batch-film by series, not by day** — shoot 8 episodes of #4 in one sitting with a stack of bags. This is how "banking" content actually gets made.
- **Every episode ends with a follow reason** (next in the series) AND a site nudge (filter live at thebeanshub.com).
- **The funnel job (from CLAUDE.md):** these bank *attention*; capture it. Route viewers to the email list, because right now a viral spike leaks away.
- Kill any series with no traction by episode 4. Double down on the 1–2 that pop.
