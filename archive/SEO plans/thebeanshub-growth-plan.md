# The Beans Hub — Growth Plan
*Honest. No fluff. Built for people who want to grind.*

---

## Where you actually are right now

Traffic is low because three things are all true at the same time: your domain is new so Google doesn't trust it yet, you have no backlinks so Google has no evidence it should, and your social cadence is inconsistent so you're not building an audience outside of search either. The content architecture is genuinely good. That's not the problem. The problem is that good content on a new domain with no links is invisible. You're in the hardest phase — the one where most people quit.

The honest timeline: SEO results on a new domain take 4–6 months minimum for lower-competition keywords, and 9–12 months for anything with real search volume. That is not fixable by writing more pages. It is only fixable by (a) getting other sites to link to yours, and (b) not quitting while you wait.

## 1. The foundation — fix this week

**Submit the updated sitemap to Google Search Console.**
Go to GSC → Sitemaps → submit `https://www.thebeanshub.com/sitemap.xml`. The sitemap just had 7 new Cluster 5 pages added. Google won't know they exist until you do this. Also go to URL Inspection for your 5 pillar pages and request indexing manually. This does not guarantee ranking, but it at least gets them in the queue.

**Check which pages are actually indexed.**
In GSC, go to Pages → Not indexed. Any page showing "Crawled – currently not indexed" or "Discovered – currently not indexed" needs attention. On a new domain this is common. The fix is mostly time and links, but check for any technical blockers (noindex tags, canonical issues) while you're in there.

**Verify your Core Web Vitals.**
GSC → Core Web Vitals. If any pages are flagged as "Poor", that is a ranking penalty. Static HTML sites like yours usually score well here, but worth checking.

## 2. Backlinks — the thing almost nobody does properly

This is the single most important lever and the one that requires the most unglamorous work. No backlinks = no authority = no rankings, regardless of content quality. Here is where to actually get them.

### The easiest links: get your roasters to link back to you

Every roaster listed on The Beans Hub has a website. Most have an "as seen in" or "featured on" section, or at minimum a footer or blog. Reach out to every single roaster you have listed with a personal message — not a template — telling them their beans are on the platform and asking if they'd add a link. Even a simple "Find us on The Beans Hub" link from 20 roaster sites is 20 referring domains, which at this stage is genuinely meaningful. This is the lowest-effort high-return link building available to you and almost no one does it.

### Malaysian food and lifestyle bloggers

There are hundreds of Malaysian food bloggers writing about cafés, coffee, and local brands. Most of them have domain authority you don't have yet. The play: identify the 50 most active ones, read their recent content, then reach out with a specific personalised message — something like "I noticed you wrote about [roaster X] last month, they're on The Beans Hub now, thought you might want to include a mention." Give them something useful, not a generic link request. Even if 5 out of 50 say yes, that's 5 referring domains.

Tools: Google "best coffee Malaysia blog", "café hopping Malaysia", "Malaysian food blogger". Also check who is linking to your competitors using Ahrefs free tier or Moz Link Explorer.

### Xiaohongshu and Instagram accounts with websites

Many coffee content creators in Malaysia have both a social presence and a website or blog. The website is where the link lives. Find them, engage genuinely with their content for a few weeks, then reach out. This also builds your social presence as a side effect.

### Get into local roundup articles

Search for "best coffee Malaysia 2026", "specialty coffee Malaysia guide", "where to buy coffee beans Malaysia". For every article that ranks on page 1 that doesn't mention The Beans Hub, email or DM the author. Be direct: "You wrote about buying specialty coffee in Malaysia — The Beans Hub is the only directory that lists all Malaysian-roasted specialty beans in one place, might be worth a mention." Roundup articles update. Even one mention in a high-traffic piece is worth hundreds of generic backlinks.

### SAYS, Vulcan Post, Coconuts KL, Buro Malaysia, TheEdge

These are the publications Malaysians actually read. They write about local businesses, F&B, startup scenes. The Beans Hub is a legitimate story — first specialty coffee bean directory in Malaysia, built for home brewers. Pitch it as a story, not as a request for a backlink. A single article in Vulcan Post or SAYS would send more referral traffic and authority than a month of other link building. Write a two-paragraph pitch and send it. The worst they say is nothing.

### Free business directories and coffee directories

Boring but real links: Malaysia business directories (FourSquare, Foursquare Places, local chamber of commerce sites), Google Business Profile (set this up if you haven't — it's free authority and shows up in map results), Crunchbase (you're a startup), Product Hunt (launch there). None of these individually move the needle much but collectively they add up and they are all free.

## 3. The supply side — this is actually the product

**The catalogue depth is the product.** If someone comes to The Beans Hub and sees 8 roasters, they leave unimpressed. If they see 60 roasters with beans fully tagged by taste note, origin, process and roast level, they bookmark it and come back. Every roaster you add makes the platform more useful to every future visitor. This is the flywheel.

Most people building a marketplace spend too much time on marketing before the product is deep enough to retain anyone. Prioritise getting roasters listed over almost everything else right now. Set a target: 50 roasters within 3 months. That number gives the platform enough depth to feel like a real directory rather than a side project.

How to actually get roasters listed:
- DM every specialty roaster in Malaysia on Instagram. Start with KL, then Penang, then JB, then everywhere else. Be direct and personal — tell them specifically why their beans would be a good fit.
- Attend any local coffee events, pop-ups, or markets where roasters show up. In-person conversations convert faster than DMs.
- Offer to write a short roaster profile or feature for free in exchange for letting you list their beans. This gives them something and builds the content side simultaneously.
- When roasters join, ask them to share the listing. Their audience is your target audience.

## 4. Social media — what actually works vs. what doesn't

**Instagram:**
The accounts that grow in the Malaysian coffee space are not the ones posting the prettiest flat-lays. They are the ones being genuinely useful and specific. "This natural Ethiopian from [Roaster] tastes like strawberry jam and hits different on a V60" will always outperform a generic "good morning coffee vibes" post. Specificity is the differentiator.

What works: naming actual roasters, naming actual beans, tasting notes people can verify, honest opinions (including "this one didn't work for me"), behind-the-scenes of the platform (show the work, show the discovery process), before-and-after content ("I thought I didn't like light roast until I tried this"). Reels outperform static posts. Carousels outperform single images.

What doesn't work: posting to post. Generic lifestyle imagery. Posting three times in a week then going quiet for two weeks. Hashtag-stuffing. Buying followers.

The uncomfortable reality: you probably need to post every single day, or at minimum five days a week, for six months before the account gets traction. That is the grind. Most people post for a month, see low numbers, and slow down. The accounts that win are the ones still posting at month seven when the algorithm starts rewarding them.

**Xiaohongshu (Little Red Book):**
This is the most underused channel for Malaysian Chinese specialty coffee content. The platform actively surfaces niche, specific, recommendation-style content. A post titled "我在马来西亚找到了最全的精品咖啡豆网站" (I found the most comprehensive specialty coffee bean site in Malaysia) with specific roasters, taste notes and a link in bio would perform well with almost zero competition. The content style that works on XHS is personal recommendation, not brand broadcast. Write as a person who found something interesting, not as a brand promoting itself.

## 5. Community infiltration — the free traffic most people skip

**Facebook groups.** There are multiple active Malaysian coffee groups on Facebook with thousands of members: "Coffee Lovers Malaysia", "Specialty Coffee Malaysia", "Home Barista Malaysia" and similar. Join all of them. Do not post links to your site for the first two to four weeks. Instead, answer questions genuinely. "Where can I buy good Ethiopian beans in KL?" — answer with specific recommendations, then mention that The Beans Hub has a filter for that. You become trusted, then your links become valuable.

**Reddit.** r/coffee has 4 million members globally. When someone asks "what are some good single-origin Malaysian roasters?" or "best coffee beans for V60?", a well-researched reply that mentions The Beans Hub as a resource is completely legitimate and earns traffic. This takes time to set up karma, but it's sustainable and free.

**Telegram coffee communities.** Malaysia has active Telegram groups for home brewers and coffee enthusiasts. Find them. Same approach as Facebook — contribute first, promote later.

**WhatsApp groups.** Harder to get into, but if you're connected to the local coffee scene these exist. Being the person in a group who genuinely knows about beans and can point people to a resource is a long-term play that compounds.

## 6. Email list — the only channel you actually own

You have no email list. This is the biggest structural gap because social reach is rented and search is unpredictable. An email list of 2,000 people who care about Malaysian specialty coffee is worth more than 10,000 Instagram followers.

**What to build as the lead magnet:**
A "New Beans This Month" digest — every month, three to five newly listed beans from Malaysian roasters with tasting notes and who they're for. People who sign up want discovery. Give them discovery on a schedule. This also becomes a reason to keep adding roasters to the platform — every new listing is potential newsletter content.

**Where to put the signup:**
Every blog post (in a callout, not buried at the bottom), the homepage above the fold, the shop page sidebar, the library page header. Don't put it in the footer and call it done.

**The realistic target:** 100 subscribers in the first 30 days if you actively promote it. 500 by month three if you're consistent. 2,000 by month six if the content is good. This is the owned asset that makes everything else sustainable.

## 7. Content gaps that still exist

Even with 5 clusters built, there are pages that would attract links and traffic that you don't have:

**Pages about specific Malaysian roasters** (not just listings — actual editorial profiles). "Who is [Roaster Name] and why their Ethiopia is one of the best in Malaysia right now" is the kind of content that (a) the roaster will share, (b) earns links, and (c) is impossible for a generic site to produce. You have the access and context to write these. Most directories don't bother.

**"Coffee in [city]" guides beyond KL, Penang, JB.** Ipoh has a growing specialty scene. Seremban. Kuching. These cities have lower competition in search and real local audiences. A "specialty coffee Ipoh" page with actual roasters and cafés earns local links and local traffic that your current pages don't touch.

**Video content transcribed to pages.** If you ever make YouTube or Reels content about specific beans or roasters, transcribe and expand it into a blog post. Double the use of every piece of content.

**FAQ and comparison content.** "Is [Roaster A] or [Roaster B] better for espresso?" sounds niche but these are real searches people do. Comparison content earns links and converts well because the person searching is in buying mode.

## 8. What not to do

**Don't spend money on paid social ads right now.** You have nothing to convert them into — no email capture, thin catalogue, no retention mechanism. Ads to a cold audience at this stage are money burned.

**Don't obsess over Instagram follower count.** It's a vanity metric that tells you nothing useful for the first year. Watch engagement rate and link clicks instead.

**Don't write more cluster content.** The 5 clusters are done. The next content priority is roaster profiles and city guides, not more educational posts. You have enough educational content to build authority — now you need referral content that roasters and local outlets will actually share.

**Don't chase keyword volume over keyword fit.** "Coffee" has enormous volume and zero chance of ranking. "Best washed Ethiopia beans Malaysia" has 20 searches a month and a real chance of ranking in 3 months. Small, winnable keywords that convert the right person beat unwinnable big keywords every time.

**Don't measure SEO results for the first 90 days.** It will look like nothing is working because nothing is, yet. The machine is being built. Check GSC at month 3 and month 6, not weekly.

## 9. The actual weekly schedule

This is what grinding looks like in practice.

**Every day:**
- Post one piece of content on Instagram or XHS. Not a campaign, just a real thing you noticed about a bean or a roaster.
- Spend 15 minutes in Facebook groups or Telegram answering questions.

**Every week:**
- Reach out to 5 new roasters about listing their beans.
- Send 5 personalised emails or DMs to Malaysian food bloggers or content creators about a potential mention or link.
- Check GSC for any new impressions or clicks on the cluster pages — this is your early signal that pages are being discovered.

**Every month:**
- Write one roaster profile or city guide. This is your link-earning content.
- Send your email newsletter if you have subscribers, or set it up if you don't.
- Update any pages where roaster listings have changed.
- Resubmit the sitemap to GSC if you've added new pages.

**Every quarter:**
- Review GSC Search Performance. Which pages are getting impressions but low clicks? Improve the title tags. Which pages have zero impressions? They need links.
- Audit the top 10 ranking pages for "specialty coffee Malaysia" and "coffee beans Malaysia" queries. Are they worse than yours? Can you reach out to whoever links to them and ask for a mention?
- Reach out to one journalist or publication with a genuine story pitch.

The content is built. The architecture is solid. The next 6 months are not about building more pages — they are about distribution, backlinks, and supply depth. None of that is exciting.
