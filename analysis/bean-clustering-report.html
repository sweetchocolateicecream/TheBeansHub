Read this first — the honest headline. The catalogue does not contain hidden exotic segments. When clustered on everything at once, the algorithm largely rediscovers origin region + blend-vs-single-origin — the structure your shop categories already use (silhouette ≈ 0.16, i.e. weak separation beyond geography). The real, strong structure is in taste-note combinations (silhouette 0.48–0.68): beans fall into 9 clean taste archetypes because taste categories are discrete labels. The most actionable findings are the taste archetypes (§3), the price–taste segments (§5), and the 3 distinct roaster strategy groups (§6).

1 · Data caveats before trusting anything below

beans.csv holds 775 rows, not the ~851 CLAUDE.md claims — worth reconciling with beans.json. 325 beans (42%) have Unknown roast level and 114 have no processing method; those fields carry far less signal than they should. One price was mis-formatted ("RM 1,000" — Panama Esmeralda Geisha, HisBrew); parsed correctly here. Prices span RM 16–1,000 per 200g; the RM 400+ tail is real (Geishas, Eugenioides).

5 · Price × taste segments (k=8)

Price does not track taste archetype — fruity beans span RM 18 to RM 740. The premium tail is driven by variety/lot (Geisha, competition lots), not flavour category. Practical read: "taste-matched picks under RM 50" is a viable content/email angle in every archetype.

7 · Taste mix by origin region

Africa skews Fruity+Floral, Asia-Pacific carries the Nutty/earthy and fermented profiles, multi-region blends are the Nutty/Cocoa+Sweet home. Nothing shocking — but it validates that origin-based category pages can honestly carry taste-led copy.

8 · What I'd do with this

1) Use the 9 taste archetypes as the spine of the taste-finder (/find-your-beans) — they're clean, exhaustive, and map to your existing icon set. 2) Fix the data debt: 42% Unknown roast level makes roast a near-useless filter dimension. 3) The under-RM50 fruity segment is huge — an obvious lead-magnet angle ("10 fruity Malaysian-roasted beans under RM50"). 4) Reconcile the 775 vs 851 record-count gap between CSV and JSON.
